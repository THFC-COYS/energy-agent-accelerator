Energy and Utilities Agentic Transformation Accelerator

A utility runs two clocks at once: the storm clock, measured in minutes and mutual assistance crews, and the planning clock, measured in decades and regulatory filings. Agents can work both clocks, forecasting, drafting, and watching, but the grid itself answers to licensed operators and control room authority. This accelerator packages the patterns, the readiness assessment, and the governance pack a utility needs to run agents on Google Cloud with critical infrastructure discipline.

A working reference artifact by Greg Lucas, founder of AIccountable and creator of agentlint. One rule governs every pattern on this page: agents predict, prepare, and draft. They never operate the grid. Switching, dispatch, and protection decisions stay with authorized humans, always.

Why energy and utilities, why now

The grid is being asked to do new tricks with old assets: integrate renewables and storage, survive harder weather, electrify transport and heat, and explain all of it to regulators and customers. The knowledge to run it lives in control rooms, field crews, and filing cabinets of precedent. Agents carry that knowledge further: outage risk scored feeder by feeder before the storm, crew packets assembled before the truck rolls, forecasts and filings drafted from governed data. What agents must never do is act on the grid itself.

This site runs that play in the open. Four patterns are mapped service by service to Google Cloud: Vertex AI and Gemini for prediction and drafting, BigQuery for the operational data foundation, Google Earth Engine for the geospatial and vegetation layer, and Google grade security controls throughout. An interactive assessment scores your utility across the Identify to Prove arc, and the governance section maps agentlint style checks to NERC CIP aware controls and critical infrastructure human authority.

Four transformation patterns

Each pattern names the operational outcome, the agent design, and the Google Cloud services it runs on. Every pattern ships with the same governance spine: a hard architectural boundary between agent systems and operational technology, with agents reading through governed interfaces and writing only to business systems, human authorization for anything operational, and complete logging suitable for regulatory review.

1. Grid Outage Prediction Agent

Before and during weather events, the agent scores outage risk by feeder and circuit: it fuses forecast weather, vegetation and asset condition data, historical outage patterns, and real time reports, then drafts the staging plan, likely damage areas, crew counts, and customer communication drafts. Control room and storm leadership decide staging and switching. The agent's output is a recommendation with its evidence attached.

Outcome: crews and material staged closer to the damage before it happens, estimated restoration times grounded in evidence, and post storm reviews that start from data instead of memory.

Stack: Vertex AI for the risk models and Gemini for briefing drafting, Google Earth Engine for vegetation, terrain, and storm track geospatial analysis, BigQuery for outage history, asset, and weather data products, Looker for storm room dashboards, Pub/Sub for event ingestion, Cloud Logging for the decision record.

2. Field Crew Copilot

Crews get a packet, not a scavenger hunt: the copilot assembles the job briefing, asset history, switching notes as written by operations, safety procedures, permits, and the customer context for the route, answers procedure questions from approved manuals with citations, and drafts the closeout documentation from the crew's notes. Energized work procedures are followed as written; the copilot informs, it never clears a job as safe.

Outcome: windshield time and paperwork time fall, closeouts complete the same day, and every crew works from the same current procedures.

Stack: Vertex AI Agent Builder for the crew assistant, Gemini for manual grounded answers and closeout drafting, Vertex AI Search over approved procedures and manuals, BigQuery for asset and work history, Google Maps Platform for routing context, offline capable mobile delivery for low coverage areas.

3. Energy Forecasting Pipeline

Load, renewable generation, and price forecasting as a governed pipeline instead of a spreadsheet ritual: the pipeline assembles weather, calendar, DER, and market inputs, runs the forecast ensemble, explains the drivers of each run in plain language, and publishes versioned forecasts with confidence bands to the teams that plan, trade, and report on them.

Outcome: forecast cycles shrink from days to hours, every number carries its inputs and version, and planning arguments move from whose spreadsheet to what the evidence says.

Stack: Vertex AI forecasting and custom models, BigQuery ML for baseline models and BigQuery as the governed data foundation, Google Earth Engine and weather data for geospatial inputs, Dataflow for streaming preparation, Looker for forecast review and variance reporting.

4. Regulatory Reporting Assistant

Filings, rate case support, and compliance reporting drafted from governed data with every figure traceable: the assistant assembles the data exhibits, drafts narrative sections from approved language, checks consistency across filings, and maintains the evidence index auditors and intervenors will eventually ask for. Regulatory and legal staff own every submission. Nothing files itself.

Outcome: filing cycles shorten, consistency errors surface before submission instead of during discovery, and the evidence trail is a byproduct, not a project.

Stack: Vertex AI Agent Builder for the reporting workflow, Gemini for narrative drafting and consistency checks, BigQuery for the reporting data products, Vertex AI Search over prior filings and orders, Cloud DLP for sensitive data handling, Cloud Audit Logs across the pipeline.

          The first 90 days, the way a utility transformation team would run it

          • Days 1 to 30, Identify and charter: run the census with operations, compliance, cybersecurity, and regulatory leadership in the room. State the no autonomous grid action rule in writing. Pick one pattern, outage prediction or the field crew copilot, and sign its charter before any build starts.
          • Days 31 to 60, Design and prove the data: stand up the BigQuery data products and Earth Engine layers the pattern needs behind the OT boundary. Build the proof of concept on Vertex AI with evaluation gates, including storm scenario tests. Train the first cohort of operators and crews.
          • Days 61 to 90, Implement and measure: pilot in one region or one storm season workflow with human authority on every operational decision and full logging. Package the pattern pack, and report Prove metrics, prediction accuracy, restoration outcomes, and overrides, to the governance body.
